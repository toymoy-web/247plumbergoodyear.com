Common Problems

The Repairs We Get Called For Most.

Running Or Leaking Toilets.

A worn flapper or fill valve is the usual cause of a toilet that runs constantly.

Dripping Faucets.

Worn cartridges or washers, more common on older fixtures in original Goodyear homes.

Low Water Pressure.

Often scale buildup inside aerators and older supply lines, tied back to the area's hard water.

Loose Or Leaking Fixtures.

Sinks, tubs, and shower valves that have started to seep at the connection.

Because natural gas is the dominant water heater fuel across Goodyear's master-planned communities, we also see a fair number of general repair calls tied to gas line fittings and shutoff valves. These are usually around water heaters and range hookups, alongside the standard fixture repairs every home eventually needs.

Most general repair calls in Goodyear fall into a short list: a toilet that won't stop running, a faucet that drips no matter how tight the handle is turned, or pressure that's dropped off in one part of the house but not another. The fix is usually straightforward once we're on site, which is why we can typically complete the repair the same visit instead of scheduling a return trip. Homes in the older sections south of I-10 tend to need cartridge and valve replacements more often simply because the original fixtures are older, while newer builds in Palm Valley more often see repairs tied to how appliances were connected during construction. We see the same split in Litchfield Park, where the historic core runs older fixtures than the newer streets nearby. Either way, the goal on a general repair call is the same: fix what's actually broken, explain what caused it, and leave the fixture working the way it should without turning a small job into a bigger bill than it needs to be. That's true whether the call comes from Goodyear itself or one of the nearby communities we also cover.
