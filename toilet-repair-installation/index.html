Common Problems

Signs You Need Toilet Repair

Running Constantly

A worn flapper or fill valve is the most common cause of a toilet that won't stop running.

Rocking Or Leaking At The Base

Usually a failed wax ring, sometimes a cracked flange underneath it.

Won't Fully Flush Or Clogs Often

Can be a clog in the trap, or in some cases a partial blockage further down the line.

Large Water Leak Near The Wall

A supply line or shutoff valve failure behind the toilet, worth an emergency call.

Older toilets in Goodyear's original neighborhoods south of I-10 are more likely to need a wax ring or flange repair simply due to age, while newer homes in Palm Valley and Estrella Mountain Ranch more often call about a fill valve or flapper on a builder-grade unit. A running toilet is also one of the more common causes behind an unexplained jump in the water bill.

FAQs

Common Questions

Can you fix a toilet that keeps running?

Yes, that's typically a flapper or fill valve replacement, usually bundled with any other general repair needs in the same visit.

Do you install new toilets for bathroom remodels?

Yes, we handle new installation as well as repair.

Is a toilet leaking at the base an emergency?

It can cause floor damage over time, so it's worth a prompt call even if it's not actively flooding.

Do you haul away the old toilet during a replacement?

Yes, removal and disposal is included with installation.

Can a running toilet actually affect my water bill?

Yes, a constantly running toilet can waste a meaningful amount of water over a billing cycle, often more than people expect.

A toilet that's merely annoying, like one that runs for a few seconds after every flush, is easy to put off. The problem is that a running toilet wastes water every single day it goes unfixed, and a base leak that seems minor can quietly damage subflooring long before it's obvious from the bathroom floor. Both are quick, inexpensive fixes caught early, and more involved once they're not. If you're replacing an older toilet anyway, a newer water-efficient model is worth asking about, since it can meaningfully reduce water use without any noticeable difference in day-to-day performance.
