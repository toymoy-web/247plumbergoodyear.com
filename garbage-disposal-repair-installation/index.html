Common Problems

Signs You Need Disposal Repair.

Humming But Not Spinning.

Usually a jam that can be cleared, sometimes a failed motor if clearing it doesn't help.

Leaking Underneath.

Often the sink flange seal or the dishwasher connection line, both repairable.

Frequent Clogs Or Backups.

Can point to a disposal issue or a drain line problem further down, worth diagnosing together.

Unusual Noise Or Burning Smell.

A sign the motor is struggling, worth a same-day look before it fails completely.

Disposal leaks tied to the dishwasher connection line come up often enough in Goodyear kitchens that we usually check that connection first on any disposal call involving water underneath the sink, before assuming the disposal unit itself is the source. It's a small diagnostic step that saves a second visit when the actual cause turns out to be a loose hose clamp rather than the disposal, and the same connection is worth a look during any sink repair nearby.

FAQs

Common Questions.

My disposal hums but won't spin, can that be fixed?

Usually yes, that's typically a jam we can clear rather than a failed unit, the same kind of quick fix as a stuck faucet valve.

Do you install disposals I've purchased myself?

Yes, we install customer-supplied units as well as ones we provide.

Is a leaking disposal always the disposal's fault?

Not always, the dishwasher connection line is a common culprit we check first.

How long does a disposal replacement take?

Most replacements are completed within the same visit.

Do garbage disposals eventually wear out even without a specific problem?

Yes, most units have a working lifespan of roughly 8-12 years, and age alone is a reasonable factor when deciding on repair versus replacement.

Can a disposal issue be related to what's being put down it?

Often, yes. Fibrous foods and grease are common causes of jams that have nothing to do with the unit's age or condition.

A disposal that hums without spinning is one of the more satisfying quick fixes in plumbing, since clearing a jam usually takes minutes once the unit is safely powered down and opened up. The calls that take longer are almost always leaks, because tracing water back to its actual source under a crowded sink cabinet takes more care than the repair itself once it's found. Units that hum without spinning are usually salvageable, but a disposal that's tripped its internal reset button repeatedly, smells like it's burning, or has visibly corroded housing is generally past the point where repair makes financial sense over replacement. If the disposal shares a drain line with a slow kitchen sink, that's worth mentioning when you call so we can check the drain line at the same time.
