Living in a 1990s Goodyear Home? What PebbleCreek and Palm Valley Homeowners Should Watch For

By the Goodyear 24/7 Plumbing team

Talk To A Plumber
(623) 301-9042
Call Now

PebbleCreek and the original sections of Palm Valley represent some of Goodyear's earliest master-planned development, with construction dating back to the 1990s and early 2000s. Homes from that era are now old enough that certain plumbing components are reaching the end of their expected service life, often around the same time within a given neighborhood, since most homes in a section were built within a few years of each other.

Copper Supply Lines Reaching Pinhole-Leak Age

Copper pipe generally lasts 20 to 50 years depending on water conditions, installation quality, and water pressure, though Goodyear's hard water pushes homes toward the shorter end of that range. A home built in the mid-1990s is now within the window where original copper supply lines can develop pinhole leaks, small points of internal corrosion that eventually penetrate the pipe wall.

These leaks often show up first as a slab leak, since much of the home's supply plumbing runs under the concrete foundation. A warm spot on the floor, an unexplained water bill increase, or the sound of running water with everything off are the earliest signs, often appearing well before any visible water damage.

Original Water Heaters Are Long Past Replacement

If a home still has its original water heater from initial construction, that unit is decades past its expected 10 to 15 year lifespan and has likely already been replaced at least once, possibly twice. If you're not sure when your water heater was last replaced, the manufacture date is stamped on a label on the unit itself, and checking it is worth five minutes if you don't already know.

Galvanized Fittings In Some Original Construction

While copper was standard for most 1990s Goodyear construction, some original fittings and short connector sections used galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside in a way that's not visible from outside the pipe. A galvanized fitting failing is often what causes a sudden pressure drop or a burst at a specific connection point rather than along a length of straight pipe.

What This Means For A Water Softener Decision

If your home falls into this age range and doesn't already have a whole-home water softener, installing one now won't reverse damage that's already occurred, but it will slow the ongoing corrosion rate in whatever original copper is still functioning well. For a home already showing some age-related wear, this is one of the more cost-effective ways to extend the useful life of the plumbing that hasn't failed yet.

This same pattern plays out even more visibly in Sun City, developed decades earlier in the 1960s, where original single-story construction means the entire community is now well past the age where this kind of wear is common rather than exceptional. PebbleCreek and Palm Valley are following the same trajectory, simply a few decades behind.

A Practical Inspection Checklist

If your home was built in the 1990s or early 2000s, check your water heater's manufacture date, look for any warm or damp spots on flooring near where hot water lines likely run, and consider having a plumber do a walkthrough inspection even without an active symptom. Catching a fitting that's close to failing before it actually bursts is a meaningfully smaller repair than responding to an active emergency, both in terms of cost and disruption to your household.

What To Do If You Notice Early Water Damage

If a warm spot, damp patch, or musty smell in an older section of the home turns out to be water damage in progress, a few steps help limit it while you arrange a repair. Shut off water to the affected fixture or the home's main valve if the source isn't isolated to one fixture. Avoid walking on visibly wet flooring more than necessary, since it can spread moisture further. Photograph the damage before any cleanup begins, and run a fan or open a window in the area if it's safe to do so, to slow mold growth while you wait for a technician to arrive.

What A Walkthrough Inspection Actually Covers

A preventive inspection typically includes checking visible supply lines and shutoff valves for corrosion or mineral buildup, testing water pressure at an outdoor spigot to confirm it's within a safe range for aging pipe, and reviewing the water heater's age and condition against its expected remaining service life. None of this requires opening up walls or flooring, since the goal is spotting early warning signs in what's already accessible.

For a home with a suspected but unconfirmed slab leak, this is also the point where electronic leak detection makes sense as a next step, rather than waiting for the symptom to become obvious enough that damage has already started. Catching a slab leak early, while it's still a small point of failure, keeps the repair limited to a small section of flooring rather than a larger area.

How This Compares To Homes Built More Recently

Newer construction elsewhere in Goodyear, including current-phase Estrella Mountain Ranch and Palm Valley development, uses PEX plumbing almost exclusively, which doesn't share copper's specific pinhole-leak failure mode. That doesn't mean newer homes are immune to plumbing issues, just that the specific pattern described here applies most directly to the original 1990s and early 2000s construction rather than the community as a whole today, decades later, once that original construction has had enough time to genuinely age.

The Cost Of Waiting Versus Acting Early

A pinhole leak caught early, before it's caused visible floor damage, is typically a straightforward, contained repair. The same leak left unaddressed for months can progress to warped flooring, mold growth under carpet or tile, and in some cases structural concerns if water has been undermining soil beneath the foundation for an extended period of time. The dollar difference between these two outcomes is usually significant, which is the practical argument for a proactive inspection rather than waiting for symptoms to force the issue, especially in a home that already fits the age profile described here in detail.
