The Real Reason Your Water Bill Jumped With Nothing Obviously Wrong

By the Goodyear 24/7 Plumbing team

Call Now

A water bill that's noticeably higher than usual, with no new pool, no guests staying over, no obvious change in habits, is one of the more unsettling things a homeowner can open in the mail. The good news is that the cause is almost always findable with a methodical check, rather than a mystery that requires guessing.

Start With The Easiest Explanation First

A running toilet is the single most common cause of an unexplained bill increase, and it's also the easiest to miss, since a worn flapper often runs silently rather than making an audible sound. Check each toilet by lifting the tank lid and watching whether water is trickling into the overflow tube, or drop food coloring into the tank and check the bowl ten minutes later without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, that toilet is leaking.

Outdoor irrigation is the second most overlooked cause. A stuck valve or a cracked line can run water into the ground with no visible puddle, particularly in landscaped areas where the water simply soaks into loose soil. If you have an irrigation system, check each zone manually rather than trusting the timer display, since a valve can fail open even when the controller shows it as off.

Compare Against A Real Baseline, Not A Guess

Pull your last four to six water bills if you have them accessible online through your utility's portal. A single higher month could be a seasonal factor, like increased outdoor watering during a hot stretch. A sustained increase across multiple billing periods, or a sudden jump that doesn't correspond to any seasonal pattern, points more strongly toward an actual leak rather than normal variation.

This is also where knowing which of Goodyear's four water utilities serves your address matters, since billing cycles and rate tiers differ slightly between providers. A jump that looks alarming under one utility's structure might be a predictable seasonal tier change under another, so it's worth understanding your specific provider's billing pattern before assuming the worst.

The Meter Test

With every fixture in the house off, including anything automatic, check your water meter's leak indicator, a small dial or gear-shaped icon that moves whenever water flows through the line. If it's moving with everything confirmed off, water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and your home's fixtures, or somewhere inside a wall or under the slab you haven't identified yet.

When It's Time To Call For Leak Detection

If you've ruled out toilets, irrigation, and any other automatic water use, and the meter is still moving or the bill increase remains unexplained across multiple months, that's the point where electronic leak detection makes sense. Acoustic and thermal equipment can locate a leak that's genuinely hidden, whether that's under a slab, behind a wall, or in the service line between the meter and the house.

Detection is billed separately from repair specifically so you get a clear answer on the actual cause and location before committing to any repair cost. In most cases, finding the leak is the harder part. Once it's located, the repair itself is usually straightforward.

What Not To Do

Avoid disputing a high bill with your utility before actually confirming whether a leak exists on your side of the meter. Utilities can confirm the meter itself is reading accurately, but they generally can't tell you whether the water is being used by a running toilet, a stuck irrigation valve, or a leak in your home's plumbing, since all of that happens after their meter has already recorded the usage.

How Long A Leak Can Run Before It's Noticed

Without a monthly bill review, a moderate leak can run for two to three billing cycles before the pattern becomes obvious enough to prompt action, and by then the wasted water and any associated damage have both had time to accumulate. This is part of why checking your bill against the prior month, not just against what feels normal, catches problems faster than relying on a general sense of whether the number seems high.

Smart meters, where your utility provides one, often include an app or online portal showing hourly or daily usage rather than just a monthly total. If yours does, checking for a small but constant baseline usage overnight, when nothing in the house should be running, is one of the fastest ways to catch a slow leak before it shows up as a surprising bill weeks later.

What A Technician Checks That You Might Not Think To

Beyond the obvious fixtures, a water softener that's stuck mid-regeneration cycle, a water heater's pressure relief valve venting slightly, or a small leak at a washing machine's supply connection are all places we check that a homeowner doesn't always think to look. None of these are dramatic, visible problems, which is exactly why they can run for a while before anyone notices the cumulative effect on a bill, sometimes for a full billing cycle or more.

A Simple Habit Worth Adopting

Check your water meter's leak indicator every few months, even without a specific reason to suspect a problem, the same way you might test smoke detectors on a schedule rather than waiting for a battery to die at an inconvenient time. It takes less than five minutes and catches problems while they're still small and inexpensive to fix.
