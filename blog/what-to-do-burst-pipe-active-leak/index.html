What To Do the Moment You Discover a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

By the Goodyear 24/7 Plumbing team

Talk To A Plumber
(623) 301-9042
Call Now

A burst pipe rarely announces itself politely. Most people discover one either by hearing running water somewhere it shouldn't be, or by finding standing water on a floor with no obvious source yet. What you do in the first five minutes matters more than almost any decision that follows, because it's the difference between a contained repair and a much larger water damage problem.

Step One: Shut Off The Water

Every home has a main water shutoff valve, usually located where the water line enters the house, near the water meter, or in a garage or utility area. Turning this off stops water from continuing to flow to the burst pipe, regardless of which specific line failed. If you don't know where yours is located, finding out now, before an emergency, is worth five minutes of your time.

If the burst is isolated to a single fixture, like a supply line under a sink, and that fixture has its own shutoff valve, turning off just that valve may be enough without shutting down water to the entire house. But if you're unsure or the water is spreading quickly, shutting off the main valve is the safer default.

Step Two: Address The Electrical Risk

Standing water near outlets, appliances, or a water heater's electrical connections is a real hazard. If water is pooling anywhere near an electrical panel, outlet, or plugged-in appliance, and you can safely reach your home's main electrical shutoff without stepping into the water, turning off power to the affected area is worth doing before anything else. If reaching it safely isn't possible, wait for a professional rather than risking contact with water and electricity together.

Step Three: Call For Emergency Service

Once the water is off, the immediate danger has passed, and the situation becomes a repair rather than an emergency in progress. This is the point to call, since a technician can be dispatched while you handle the next step below, rather than waiting until everything is cleaned up first. Our emergency line runs 24 hours a day for exactly this kind of call.

Step Four: Start Documenting And Mitigating

If you have a phone handy, photograph the affected area before you start moving anything, since this documentation matters if you end up filing an insurance claim. After that, move furniture, rugs, and belongings away from standing water where you safely can, and use towels or a wet vacuum if you have one to limit how far the water spreads while you wait for a technician.

Avoid running any other water in the house until the technician arrives and confirms it's safe to do so, even from an unaffected fixture, since you don't yet know the full extent of the damage to the supply system.

What Happens When A Technician Arrives

The first step on-site is confirming the water is fully shut off and assessing the extent of the damage, not immediately starting repairs. From there, the fix depends on what actually failed: a straightforward pipe section replacement, a fitting repair, or in some cases identifying that the visible burst is a symptom of a larger issue, like a section of galvanized pipe that's failed at one point and is likely to fail elsewhere soon.

Why Older Goodyear Homes See This More Often

Original galvanized and copper plumbing in some of Goodyear's older neighborhoods, and in nearby Litchfield Park's historic core, is old enough that a burst pipe is sometimes the first visible sign of broader corrosion throughout the system, rather than an isolated incident. When that's the case, we'll walk through whether spot-repairing the current break is enough or whether a broader look at the home's supply lines makes sense before the next section fails.

The One Thing Worth Doing Before You Ever Need This

Locate your main shutoff valve today, if you haven't already, and make sure everyone in the household knows where it is and how to turn it. That single piece of preparation does more to limit damage from a burst pipe than anything that happens after the water is already spreading.

What Counts As An Emergency Versus A Same-Day Call

An active burst with water actively flowing is always an emergency call, regardless of the hour. A slow drip from a fitting that's been getting slightly worse over days is worth a prompt call but doesn't carry the same urgency, since you have more control over the situation by shutting off that specific fixture's valve while you wait for a scheduled visit rather than the whole house's supply.

The distinction matters because it affects how you should respond in the moment. An active burst justifies shutting off water to the entire home immediately, even if that means no water for showers or laundry until a technician arrives. A contained, slow leak usually doesn't require that level of disruption, and isolating just the affected fixture is often enough.

After The Immediate Repair

Once the burst section is repaired and water service is restored, it's worth asking whether the failure was an isolated incident or a sign of broader wear in the home's supply lines. A single burst in an otherwise sound system is usually just that, an isolated event. A burst in a home with other symptoms, like frequent small leaks or visibly corroded exposed fittings, is worth a broader conversation about the condition of the rest of the system before the next failure happens somewhere less convenient.

Document any water damage with photos before cleanup begins, even after the repair itself is done, since insurance claims for water damage typically require this kind of documentation and it's much harder to gather convincingly after carpets have dried and drywall has been patched.
